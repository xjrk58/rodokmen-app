# Curated archive rules gate Rodokmen's European expansion

Rodokmen can reach all 27 EU states and the UK without giving up its static, offline Country packs, because the map's bulk data is cheap: every EU27+UK village from GeoNames fits in **about 5.8 MB gzipped (100–900 KB per country) under CC BY 4.0** ([GeoNames dump](https://download.geonames.org/export/dump/)), and most countries publish open gazetteers, boundaries and, increasingly, historical map services. The expensive part is the archive rules: who holds which register for which place, faith and period, and where its scans are online. No pan-European source supplies these. The current `add-country-packs` answer shape (one entry per "kind", where kind mixes denomination with civil) will not hold them either. Real answers need several holders per entry (original, duplicate, microfilm, online portal), periods that start in different years inside one Land, closure rules that move every year, and keys on historical units such as Dutch municipalities of 1812 or Belgian municipalities before the 1977 mergers. Ranked by value per effort, Sweden, Slovenia, Luxembourg, Portugal, Scotland and (for rules) France are the cheapest packs, and Bremen, North Rhine-Westphalia and Hesse are the cheapest German Länder. England, Spain, Hungary, Romania, Greece, Lithuania and Lower Saxony are the hardest. The legal changes bite hardest on the Tree, not the map. The GDPR household exemption fits a closed single-family instance. A shared multi-family service makes its operator a controller of religion-revealing data, subject to national post-mortem rules such as Denmark's 10 years ([danskelove.dk §2](https://danskelove.dk/databeskyttelsesloven/2)), and to US processing for AI reading, which Anthropic's first-party API cannot pin to the EU. The recommended path is to fold the format and Tree-model changes into `add-country-packs` now, while none of its tasks has started. Then prove the generic format on open-data countries, keep the hard ones for later, and have the Owner settle a short list of decisions first: one family or many, commercial or not, and how long a person counts as Living.

## Pan-European data covers search and basemaps, not archive answers

The research tested every Europe-wide source that could replace per-country work. The result is clear: **pan-European data solves place search and the basemap, while archive answers, historical affiliation and historical overlays stay per-country curation.**

**GeoNames is the only free, commercially usable, Europe-wide populated-place source that also covers the UK.** It is CC BY 4.0, ships per-country zips with daily modification and deletion files, and its alternate names carry a language, an `isHistoric` flag and from/to periods ([GeoNames readme](https://download.geonames.org/export/dump/readme.txt)). The researchers counted **578,277 populated places** across the 28 countries after dropping sections, historical, abandoned and destroyed places. A minimal TSV (id, name, coordinates, admin codes, feature code, population) is 29.7 MB raw, 5.8 MB gzip and 4.6 MB zstd. Per country it is CZ 164 KB, PL 426 KB, GB 365 KB, IT 675 KB, DE 710 KB and FR 857 KB gzipped. Adding alternate names doubles the total to 10.7 MB ([GeoNames dump](https://download.geonames.org/export/dump/)). The weakness is history. Only 8,195 of 16,634 Czech populated places have any alternate name, so German, Hungarian and Polish exonyms are patchy ([GeoNames CZ.zip](https://download.geonames.org/export/dump/CZ.zip)). Gramps users note that GeoNames "only has the current place structure", with no time-dependent hierarchy ([Gramps Discourse](https://gramps.discourse.group/t/places-database-for-gramps/2185)).

Municipality polygons are harder. Eurostat GISCO LAU 2024 holds **97,987 local units, but zero UK rows**: UK coverage ended after the 2020 table ([LAU 2024 CSV](https://gisco-services.ec.europa.eu/distribution/v2/lau/csv/LAU_RG_01M_2024_4326.csv); [Eurostat LAU page](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/nuts/local-administrative-units)). The files are 78 MB as GeoPackage and 150 MB as GeoJSON ([GISCO /lau/](https://gisco-services.ec.europa.eu/distribution/v2/lau/)). The licence grants a right on condition the data "will not be used for commercial purposes", and commercial users are sent to EuroGeographics ([GISCO copyright](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/gisco/geodata/statistical-units)). EuroBoundaryMap does cover the UK, but its terms conflict. The EEA's record says non-commercial and "only to be used internally at the EEA" while also naming a EuroGeographics Open Data Licence ([EEA record](https://sdi.eea.europa.eu/catalogue/srv/api/records/edc83573-94c5-4e2a-afc1-3484f710e46e)). An Icelandic fact sheet calls it "non-open", licensed for a fee ([LMI](https://lmi.is/static/files/ome2-fact-sheet.pdf)). The open-licence text itself could not be read, so treat EBM as unverified. OpenStreetMap covers everything, but the OSMF geocoding guideline treats a systematic collection of OSM places used "as a general purpose geodatabase" as a Derivative Database under ODbL share-alike ([OSMF Geocoding Guideline](https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/Licence/Community_Guidelines/Geocoding_-_Guideline)). In practice, national open data is the better polygon source in almost every country. The per-country sections below show it is usually CC BY, CC0, OGL, Licence Ouverte or dl-de/by.

Historical affiliation is where pan-European data runs out. The MPIDR Mosaic historical boundaries (Europe 1900, Austria-Hungary 1910, Poland 1770–1978) are "freely used for non-commercial scientific purposes" only ([Mosaic](https://mosaic.ipums.org/historical-gis-datafiles)). HistoGIS shows no licence or coverage on its homepage ([HistoGIS](https://histogis.acdh.oeaw.ac.at/)). The Verein für Computergenealogie's GOV gazetteer blocks automated fetches. A search snippet reports its Mini-GOV export as **CC BY-ND 4.0, which forbids the derived files a pack would be** ([GOV wiki](https://wiki.genealogy.net/GOV/Mini-GOV)). The pan-European note records the licence as unverified. Either way it cannot be shipped. The Gramps experience is a warning: its GetGOV add-on broke when GOV changed its interface, and one place could create 41 Gramps places with a defective hierarchy ([Gramps Discourse](https://gramps.discourse.group/t/getgov-import-failure/6411)). Wikidata is CC0 and could bridge GeoNames IDs to historical names and parish items ([Wikidata:Licensing](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Wikidata:Licensing)), but its parish coverage was not researched.

Register portals help only in their own regions. **Matricula is the one aggregator that answers "where are the parish scans for place X" across borders.** Its holdings page lists Germany 9,228, Austria 2,873, Slovenia 723, Switzerland 292, Luxembourg 277 and Italy 106 entries, and **Belgium is not listed**, contrary to the brief ([Matricula Bestände](https://data.matricula-online.eu/en/bestande/)). Images are CC BY-NC-ND 2.0 with no commercial reuse ([Matricula terms](https://data.matricula-online.eu/en/nutzungsbedingungen/)). Its map page loads a 2.6 MB GeoJSON of 13,481 parish points (9,218 in Germany, 2,866 in Austria) whose reuse licence is unstated ([Matricula landkarte features](https://data.matricula-online.eu/de/landkarte-features/all/)). Its place search at `/de/suchen/` takes `place`, `diocese`, `date_filter` and `date_range`. That pattern was observed but not tested end to end, and it is the first concrete candidate for the plan's open `placeSearchUrl` question ([Matricula suchen](https://data.matricula-online.eu/de/suchen/)).

FamilySearch is the de facto scan host for Slovakia, Hungary, Croatia and Spain, and partly for Romania, Bulgaria and Greece. Since 2017 its record collections require a signed-in account ([FamilySearch Help](https://www.familysearch.org/en/help/helpcenter/article/why-do-i-have-to-sign-in-to-view-historical-records)). Its 6-million-place Places API needs a developer key, a token flow and an app readiness review ([FamilySearch quick-start](https://developers.familysearch.org/main/docs/familysearch-api-quick-start-guide-bruno-edition); [checklists](https://www.familysearch.org/innovate/api-compatible-checklists)). It is therefore a link-out target, not a data source. OpenArchieven is the only aggregator with an open, keyless, CORS-enabled record API (4 requests per second per IP), mostly for the Netherlands and Belgium ([Open Archives API docs](https://www.openarchieven.nl/api/docs/)). Archives Portal Europe has a keyed JSON API whose data is available only from countries that consented, and its site returned 403. It suits build-time curation, not per-click lookups ([Archives Hub blog](https://blog.archiveshub.jisc.ac.uk/category/archives-portal-europe); [Europeana Pro](https://pro.europeana.eu/organisation/archives-portal-europe)).

No open pan-European historical map layer was found, though that is absence of evidence rather than proof. Arcanum Maps has the broadest coverage (the Habsburg military surveys and a 19th-century Europe mosaic). It sells consumer plans from EUR 12.90 a month, and its free Guest tier shows low-resolution maps only ([Arcanum plans](https://maps.arcanum.com/en/plans-and-pricing/)). No public WMTS, API or embedding licence exists. Arcanum's published terms (written for its newspaper product) ban automated downloading and require "a separate agreement" for commercial use ([Arcanum T&C](https://www.arcanum.com/en/adt/terms-and-conditions/)). Allmaps plus IIIF is the open, embeddable route. Small Georeference Annotation files can ship in packs while archives serve the pixels ([Allmaps MapLibre](https://allmaps.org/docs/packages/maplibre/)). For the basemap, the OSMF tile policy says **"Offline use is not permitted on tile.openstreetmap.org"** and bans pre-emptive fetching ([OSMF Tile Usage Policy](https://operations.osmfoundation.org/policies/tiles/)). Rodokmen uses those tiles today, with a 2,000-tile runtime cache and no prefetch (`add-map-core` design, `app/data/layers.json`). The free MapTiler and Stadia tiers are non-commercial ([MapTiler pricing](https://www.maptiler.com/cloud/pricing/); [Stadia pricing](https://stadiamaps.com/pricing/)). A self-hosted Protomaps PMTiles extract (the planet is about 120 GB at z0–15, and each extra zoom level roughly doubles the size) is the only option with no per-view fee that also allows offline use ([Protomaps](https://docs.protomaps.com/basemaps/downloads)).

## Effort per country follows record custody, not country size

The ratings below are the researchers' qualitative judgements of data availability, with sources cited per country in the subsections. No pack prototype was built, and no person-days were estimated. The pattern is consistent. A country is cheap when one public archive holds the registers, a single free portal shows them, and open geodata exists. It is expensive when custody splits by denomination, period or historic province, or when scans sit behind paywalls, logins or bot walls.

### The four planned packs need corrections, not redesign

The research answers several of the plan's open questions. **Austrian Protestant registers are already on Matricula**: Augsburg Confession collections cover Carinthia 32, Lower Austria 21, Upper Austria 20, Styria 27 and Vienna 16, plus Burgenland 29 ([Matricula Austria](https://data.matricula-online.eu/de/oesterreich/); [Wien A.B.](https://data.matricula-online.eu/de/oesterreich/wien-evang-dioezese-AB/)). Eisenstadt is not on Matricula, apart from two Heiligenkreuz parishes. Tyrol's images are in the Tiroler Landesarchiv's Matriken Tirol Online, with only Ampass and Innsbruck-Wilten hosted on Matricula ([Matricula Tirol](https://data.matricula-online.eu/de/oesterreich/tirol/)). For Poland, no change to record access or APIs after 2025 was found. The newest confirmed item is still the 2019 relaunch of the State Archives' portal with over 37 million free scans ([dzieje.pl](https://dzieje.pl/node/105264)). Records of the former German east have moved: the Standesamt I registers have been at the Landesarchiv Berlin since 2015 (births 1874–1910, marriages 1874–1940, deaths 1874–1945) ([LAB](https://landesarchiv-berlin.de/standesaemter-der-ehemaligen-deutschen-ostgebiete)). The Evangelisches Zentralarchiv holds about 6,000 books from the eastern provinces, but many Posen Kreise have no scans ([Archion EZA](https://www.archion.de/de/alle-archive/berlin-/-brandenburg/evangelisches-zentralarchiv-in-berlin)).

**Cutting Bavarian and Saxon regions along OpenStreetMap diocese boundaries will not work.** Germany has only three Catholic diocese-level `religious_administration` relations, for Aachen, Speyer and Vechta ([Geofabrik taginfo](https://taginfo.geofabrik.de/europe:germany/api/4/tag/stats?key=boundary&value=religious_administration)). The plan's fallback of listing both dioceses must be the norm, or boundaries can be derived from Matricula parish points. For Bavarian and Saxon Localities, BKG's GN250 (about 170,000 names, dl-de/by-2-0) avoids the ODbL share-alike that OSM-derived files bring ([BKG GN250](https://gdz.bkg.bund.de/index.php/default/geographische-namen-1-250-000-gn250.html)). Bavaria's historical maps stay fee-based: a 2024 open-data presentation lists "Historische Karten" under "Kostenpflichtig bleiben", so Bavaria stays link-only ([StMFH/LDBV 2024](https://lohr.bayernlab.bayern.de/mam/ldbv/dateien/infoverm_2024_opendata_stopet.pdf)). Saxony is easier than the plan assumed. GeoSN's `wms_geosn_hist` serves the pre-1945 Messtischblatt in EPSG:3857 free of charge ([GeoSN WMS](https://geodienste.sachsen.de/wms_geosn_hist/guest?REQUEST=GetCapabilities&SERVICE=WMS)). Saxony's Protestant books are released on Archion one Kirchenbezirk at a time. Pirna is reportedly on-site only from Q4 2026, a claim that comes from an unverified forum post ([Archion forum](https://www.archion.de/en/forum/threads/s%C3%A4chsische-kirchenb%C3%BCcher-bei-archion-%E2%80%93-warum-liegen-bereits-digitalisierte-best%C3%A4nde-weiterhin-offline.25625/page-4)). The Görlitz diocesan archive holds no registers at all ([Bistum Görlitz](https://www.bistum-goerlitz.de/bistumsarchiv/)). Archion has no API, its slugs change (Württemberg's moved), and permalinks reportedly redirect to image 1 ([Archion forum](https://www.archion.de/en/forum/goto/post?id=130961)). The weekly link check is therefore essential, not optional.

### Germany's other Länder hinge on civil duplicates and church borders

Germany runs three overlapping systems that match Land borders "only in exceptional cases". The Protestant church of the Rhineland alone spans NRW, Rhineland-Palatinate, Saarland and Hesse ([Statistisches Jahrbuch 1979](https://digi.bib.uni-mannheim.de/fileadmin/statjahrb/514402342_1979/pdf/514402342_0088.pdf)). Since Bremen joined on 7 April 2026, **Protestant books from all 16 Länder are on Archion** ([Archion news](https://www.archion.de/en/discover-archion/news/all-news/complete-with-bremen-church-records-from-all-federal-states-available)). Archion is paid: 19.90 € for 30 days with 50 downloads and 10 text recognitions, or 178.80 € a year ([Archion prices](https://www.archion.de/de/zugang-kaufen)). Three Catholic dioceses sit on this "Protestant" portal: Speyer, Trier and part of Hildesheim ([Archion Speyer](https://www.archion.de/de/alle-archive/rheinland-pfalz/bistumsarchiv-speyer); [Archion Trier](https://www.archion.de/de/alle-archive/rheinland-pfalz/bistumsarchiv-trier)). Freiburg, Köln, Berlin and Hamburg are not online. Paderborn stopped answering family-research requests on 1 May 2026 ([Matricula DE](https://data.matricula-online.eu/de/deutschland/)).

Civil registration began on 1 October 1874 in Prussia and on 1 January 1876 in the Reich, but in 1798 on the left bank of the Rhine and in 1850 in Anhalt ([FamilySearch time periods](https://www.familysearch.org/en/wiki/German_Civil_Registration_Time_Periods)). **NRW and Hesse are the only Länder with free, Land-wide scans of the second civil copy.** NRW's are at LAV NRW, filed by the Standesamt that existed before the 1960s–70s reforms ([PA 3103](https://www.archive.nrw.de/sites/default/files/media/files/PA3103_EinleitungDigitalisatpr%C3%A4sentationAugust21.pdf)). Hesse's are at the Personenstandsarchiv in Neustadt, online for births to 1900, marriages to 1930 and deaths to 1965, with a project to extend this by 2027 ([Hessen research guide](https://landesarchiv.hessen.de/sites/landesarchiv.hessen.de/files/2026-02/recherche_in_den_digitalisierten_personenstandsnebenregistern.pdf)). Baden-Württemberg's pre-1876 state duplicates of both confessions (Baden 1810–1870, Württemberg 1808–1875) have been on Matricula since May 2025 ([Matricula news](https://data.matricula-online.eu/de/nachrichten/baden-wuerttemberg_01/)). In the former GDR, order 110/76 (as amended in 1982) allowed second copies to be destroyed, except deaths 1933–1945, so usually only the municipal first copy survives ([BLHA 2008](https://blha.brandenburg.de/sixcms/media.php/9/Zustaendigkeit_Personenstandsunterlagen.pdf)).

Open EPSG:3857 historical WMS exist for NRW (Tranchot, Uraufnahme and Neuaufnahme under dl-de/zero), Rhineland-Palatinate, Schleswig-Holstein, Baden-Württemberg, Brandenburg, Bremen and Saxony. Mecklenburg-Vorpommern's service explicitly forbids "das Einbinden in Internetpräsentationen bzw. Apps" ([NRW Uraufnahme WMS](https://www.wms.nrw.de/geobasis/wms_nw_uraufnahme); [MV WMS](https://www.geodaten-mv.de/dienste/historische_karten_wms)). The researchers rate Bremen low, Hamburg low–medium, most Länder medium and Lower Saxony high: four Landeskirchen plus a Reformed church whose books are not online, three Hildesheim channels and no map WMS ([Archion archive list](https://www.archion.de/de/alle-archive)). Their proposed order is Bremen, NRW and Hesse first, then Rhineland-Palatinate and Baden-Württemberg, then the eastern Länder, with Lower Saxony last. The civil holders in Saarland, Saxony-Anhalt, Schleswig-Holstein, Mecklenburg-Vorpommern and Lower Saxony, and several Landeskirche territories, are unverified.

### Benelux and France have simple rules and fragmented viewers

**Luxembourg is the cleanest pack in the west.** ANLux holds civil status from 1795 in the fonds of the two district tribunals, and its scans run 1795–1912. The diocesan archive is on Matricula, and all national geodata is CC0, including localities with coordinates and 130 former communes usable as Locality rows ([ANLux guide](https://anlux.public.lu/dam-assets/pdf-statiques/guide-tat-civil-092025.pdf); [data.public.lu](https://data.public.lu/fr/datasets/liste-des-localites-avec-coordonnees/)). Its geoportail serves Ferraris and historical topographic maps in EPSG:3857 with "none" access constraints. Those layers are absent from the open-data services, though, so the embedding licence needs confirming with the agency ([geoportail.lu WMTS](https://wmts1.geoportail.lu/mapproxy_4_v3/wmts/1.0.0/WMTSCapabilities.xml)). Luxembourg also forces the first cross-border referrals. Records for places of the former Duchy now abroad are in Arlon, Koblenz or the Moselle archives ([ANLux genealogy](https://anlux.public.lu/fr/rechercher/genealogie.html)).

The Netherlands has the best API of any country but the hardest keys. Civil registration began on **1 March 1811**, with Limburg and parts of Zeeland from 1795. This conflicts with the 1796 date in the brief ([FamilySearch NL](https://www.familysearch.org/en/wiki/Netherlands_Civil_Registration)). About 45 regional archives appear in the OpenArchieven list ([archives.json](https://api.openarchieven.nl/1.1/stats/archives.json)). More than 1,100 municipalities existed in 1812 against 342 today, so rules must key on the historical municipality's Amsterdamse Code from gemeentegeschiedenis.nl, whose licence is not stated ([gemeentegeschiedenis.nl](https://www.gemeentegeschiedenis.nl/); [CBS 2026](https://www.cbs.nl/nl-nl/onze-diensten/methoden/classificaties/overig/gemeentelijke-indelingen-per-jaar/indeling-per-jaar/gemeentelijke-indeling-op-1-januari-2026)). The OpenArchieven API cannot list records by place without a name ([Open Archives API docs](https://www.openarchieven.nl/api/docs/)), and Topotijdreis serves only EPSG:28992. **Belgium** looks simple because one portal, Agatha, covers more than 28,000 parish registers to 1796 and more than 36,000 civil registers 1796–1920 ([rfgenealogie](https://www.rfgenealogie.com/infos/archives-de-l-etat-en-belgique-clap-de-fin-pour-le-moteur-de-recherche-search)). But Agatha needs a captcha-gated free account. It is keyed on about 2,900 pre-1977 municipalities without coordinates, and its searches are POST requests with session tokens ([Agatha](https://agatha.arch.be/en/search/genealogie/)). Flanders' historical maps are an open EPSG:3857 WMTS, but their exact licence is unverified ([HISTCART WMTS](https://geo.api.vlaanderen.be/HISTCART/wmts?SERVICE=WMTS&REQUEST=GetCapabilities)).

**France reverses the usual pattern: rules are trivial, deep links are hard.** Parish registers before 1792 and état civil from 1792 sit with each département's Archives départementales. The FranceArchives directory is an open CSV (Licence Ouverte, 1,312 rows, 118 AD entries, coordinates), so about 101 rules can be generated from one file ([data.gouv.fr annuaire](https://www.data.gouv.fr/datasets/portail-francearchives-annuaire-des-services-darchives-publiques-en-france)). Viewing has been free nationally since 2016 ([RFG](https://www.rfgenealogie.com/node/3995)). Each AD runs its own viewer, though. Of 19 homepages probed, **four returned a JavaScript-only bot wall and six showed captchas** ([archives.var.fr](https://archives.var.fr/)), so packs should link to AD landing pages and the link checker must tolerate bot walls. The map side is excellent. IGN's keyless WMTS serves Cassini, the 1820–1866 État-major map and the 1950 SCAN 50 under Licence Ouverte. Cassini requires a long verbatim attribution string ([geopf GetCapabilities](https://data.geopf.fr/wmts?SERVICE=WMTS&REQUEST=GetCapabilities&VERSION=1.0.0); [Cassini CSW](https://data.geopf.fr/csw?REQUEST=GetRecordById&SERVICE=CSW&VERSION=2.0.2&OUTPUTSCHEMA=http://standards.iso.org/iso/19115/-3/mdb/2.0&elementSetName=full&ID=ARCHIVES-NATIONALES_CASSINI-ROI)).

### Southern Europe pairs centralised Portugal with scattered Spain

**Portugal is the best-covered southern country.** Parish registers to 1911 were handed to the state, and DGLAB's digitarq links more than 8 million descriptions to more than 63 million free images ([digitarq](https://digitarq.arquivos.pt/)). Most district archives are 94–100% digitised; Vila Real is the outlier at 61.4% ([tombo.pt statistics](https://tombo.pt/en/content/archive-statistics)). Seven districts split their holdings with the Torre do Tombo, so both must be listed ([FamilySearch Portugal](https://www.familysearch.org/en/wiki/Portugal_Church_Records)). The excellent tombo.pt index is a private, "all rights reserved" project, so a pack must be built from digitarq instead ([tombo.pt about](https://tombo.pt/en/content/about-us)). CAOP boundaries are CC BY 4.0 ([DGT CAOP](https://www.dgterritorio.gov.pt/cartografia/cartografia-tematica/caop)), but no historical map service was found.

**Italy** has one national viewer. Antenati offers about 155 million images from 98 state-archive institutes ([DGA news](https://archivi.cultura.gov.it/archivio-notizie/notizia?tx_news_pi1%5Baction%5D=detail&tx_news_pi1%5Bcontroller%5D=News&tx_news_pi1%5Bnews%5D=2378&cHash=08c7181cada07ed13d980af6f21d612e)), using IIIF Presentation 2 behind an AWS WAF that blocks automated clients and rejects full-size requests ([gcerretani/antenati troubleshooting](https://github.com/gcerretani/antenati/blob/master/TROUBLESHOOTING.md)). Parish registers are not in Antenati: they sit with the dioceses, and the bishops' conference's BeWeB is a directory, not access ([BeWeB FAQ](https://www.beweb.chiesacattolica.it/istituticulturali/contenuti/6/Domande+frequenti)). Civil rules need a pre-unitary-state dimension: south 1809, Sicily 1820, centre and north 1866, Veneto 1871, plus Napoleonic series ([FamilySearch Italy](https://www.familysearch.org/en/wiki/Italy_Civil_Registration)). **Spain is the hardest of the five.** PARES holds 41 million images of state archives but no vital records ([PARES statistics](https://pares.cultura.gob.es/estadisticas.html)). Parish books over 100 years old sit in roughly 70 diocesan archives (count unverified), each with its own access and fees. The Basque sacramental search is reportedly available only from Spain ([FamilySearch Spain](https://www.familysearch.org/en/wiki/Spain_Church_Records)). Spain's maps and gazetteer, by contrast, are open: IGN's first-edition MTN50 (1875–1968) is a CC BY 4.0 WMTS with a GoogleMapsCompatible matrix set, and CNIG's population-entity gazetteer is CC BY 4.0 with coordinates ([IGN WMTS](https://www.ign.es/wmts/primera-edicion-mtn?request=GetCapabilities&service=WMTS); [CNIG NGMEP](https://centrodedescargas.cnig.es/CentroDescargas/nomenclator-geografico-municipios-entidades-poblacion)). **Malta** is hand-curatable, with 68 local councils and two dioceses, but its civil certificates are paid and parish scans need registration ([FamilySearch Malta](https://www.familysearch.org/en/wiki/Malta_Civil_Registration); [Malta Parish Archives](https://www.maltaparisharchives.org/about)).

### Habsburg and Balkan lands share Hungarian law and Orthodox calendars

**Slovakia is closest to the Czech model.** Church registers became state property in 1949 and moved into regional state archives from 1952, arranged by parish. Civil registers kept from 1 October 1895 under Hungarian law stay at local registry offices until 100 years after the last entry ([FamilySearch Slovakia](https://www.familysearch.org/en/wiki/Slovakia_Church_Records); [Act 154/1994](https://www.zakonypreludi.sk/print/zz/1994-154/znenie-20260801.htm)). FamilySearch's free "Slovakia Church and Synagogue Books, 1592–1935" is the online answer, with no verified per-parish deep link ([FS 1554443](https://www.familysearch.org/search/collection/1554443)). ZBGIS geodata is CC BY 4.0, but its documented WMTS is S-JTSK only ([skgeodesy](https://www.skgeodesy.sk/geoportal-en/services/web-map-services/wmts/wmts-zbgis)). **Slovenia is the easiest Habsburg country**: Ljubljana, Maribor and Koper are all on Matricula ([Matricula Slovenia](https://data.matricula-online.eu/en/slovenia/)), and GURS data is CC BY 4.0 ([GURS WMS](https://eprostor.gov.si/imps/srv/api/records/a1af8b13-2a5d-45d3-ae50-62069a2d2713)). Rules should key on the historic diocese territory, and Prekmurje, Hungarian until 1919, needs its own rule.

**Hungary is the most fragmented.** Original registers stay with each denomination's archives. The National Archives' roughly 4 million microfilm images of church registers to 1895 are viewable only in its reading rooms, per 2019 and 2023 sources ([2023 lecture](https://www.jmvk-komarom.hu/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/Csaladfakutatas-eloadas-20230209.pdf)). The 1895–1980 civil duplicates (about 22 million pages) are being put online county by county. Pest county came first, with AI handwriting recognition since December 2025 ([HVG](https://hvg.hu/tudomany/20251221_mnl-aol-leveltar-csaladfakutatas-anyakonyv-keziras-felismeressel)). FamilySearch has the broadest free images ([FamilySearch Hungary](https://www.familysearch.org/en/wiki/Hungary_Online_Genealogy_Records)), and the boundary licence is unclear. Hungary is also the hub for cross-border Habsburg-Hungarian records, and its Magyarised place names (dates reported as about 1898–1918, unverified) must appear as aliases in the Slovak, Romanian, Croatian and Slovene packs. **Croatia** splits custody by period: the State Archives in Zagreb holds most older registers, regional archives hold those to 1946, and the cut-off is given as 1857 or 1860 depending on the source ([FamilySearch Croatia](https://www.familysearch.org/en/wiki/Croatia_Church_Records); [exyugenealogy](https://exyugenealogy.net/archives-church-records/archives-church-records-in-croatia)). Its new eArhiv is a catalogue and ordering system, not a register viewer ([eArhiv](https://earhiv.gov.hr/en/about-portal)).

**Romania** has three civil regimes: the Old Kingdom from 1865, Transylvania and Banat from 1895, and Bukovina, Bessarabia and Dobruja from 1919. Registers older than about 100 years go to 42 county services plus Bucharest, which suits a county rule, but online access is thin ([FamilySearch Romania](https://www.familysearch.org/en/wiki/Romania_Civil_Registration)). The eANR digitisation was announced in 2021, and no launch was found ([EVZ](https://evz.ro/arhivele-nationale-proiect-stramosii.html)). ANCPI boundaries, including villages, are open under OGL-ROU-1.0 ([INSPIRE record](https://inspire-geoportal.ec.europa.eu/srv/api/records/0cc165fd-1e90-469c-b99a-2f99d9c964fd)). **Bulgaria** has centralised regional archives and an official, downloadable EKATTE settlement register, but its ISSA system is a Bulgarian-only finding aid with no images ([Legacy Tree](https://www.legacytree.com/blog/legacy-tree-onsite-bulgarian-state-archives); [NSI EKATTE](https://www.nsi.bg/nrnm/index.php/ekatte/index)). The archive structure is given as six regional archives with 21 centres or as 28 districts, which is unresolved. **Greece fits the pack model worst.** It has three civil-type sources (the 1925/1931 civil registry, municipal rolls from at least 1887, and male registers), spread across municipalities, the Interior Ministry and regional state-archive offices ([FamilySearch Greece](https://www.familysearch.org/en/wiki/Greece_Civil_Registration)). The 2011 reform cut municipalities from 1,033 to 325, so pre-2011 communities must be Localities ([Statoids](https://statoids.com/ygr.html)). **Cyprus** has six districts, civil registration starting between 1870 and 1915 by district, and almost nothing online ([FamilySearch Cyprus](https://www.familysearch.org/en/wiki/Cyprus_Civil_Registration)).

Two pieces of work are shared across this region. One rule template for Hungarian civil registration from 1 October 1895, with its duplicates, serves Slovakia, Hungary, Transylvania and Banat, Međimurje and Prekmurje. And the civil calendar switches (Bulgaria 1916, Romania 1919, Greece 1923) do not apply to church books, because "none of the national Eastern Orthodox Churches have recognised the Gregorian calendar for church or religious purposes" ([Wikipedia: Gregorian adoption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adoption_of_the_Gregorian_calendar)).

### Nordic and Baltic packs range from Sweden as the model to Lithuania as the stress test

**Sweden is the best case in Europe.** Riksarkivet's images have been free since 2018 and now number more than 375 million ([Riksarkivet](https://riksarkivet.se/digitisering)). Digitised records mostly carry the Public Domain Mark and metadata is CC0. The IIIF Image and Presentation 3.0 APIs need no key, but public APIs serve only material older than 110 years ([API terms](https://sok.riksarkivet.se/en/data-api/api/anvandarvillkor-api); [IIIF wiki](https://github.com/Riksarkivet/dataplattform/wiki/IIIF-english-version)). Lantmäteriet's parish polygons (about 2,350 socknar and 130 städer) allow free commercial use without attribution ([Lantmäteriet](https://lantmateriet.se/en/geodata/geodata-products/product-list/parish-and-city-download-vektor)). Historical parishes from 1600 to 1990 are reported CC0 via the histmaps package; the claim rests on the package, since Riksarkivet's own download page was not found ([histmaps](https://github.com/junkka/histmaps)). The church kept the population register until 30 June 1991, so Swedish rules need a time cut-off ([Skatteverket](https://www.skatteverket.se/privat/folkbokforing/attvarafolkbokford/folkbokforingenshistoria)). **Sweden could prove a per-parish IIIF deep link, which nothing in the Czech pack has.**

Denmark's Arkivalieronline is free but about 2% digitised, with no API, and Rigsarkivet must be asked before bulk downloads ([Rigsarkivet](https://en.rigsarkivet.dk/?p=540)). Its address and boundary services are migrating, with non-modernised Datafordeleren services closing on 15 January 2027 ([Lovguiden](https://lovguiden.dk/det-offentlige/klimadatastyrelsen/2026-06-25-datafordelerens-modernisering-paralleldrift-ophoerer-15-januar-2027)). The historical-parish atlas DigDag (more than 70,000 polygons) has an unknown licence. Finland splits images across Astia (to about the 1860s, free when over 100 years old), the genealogical society's SSHY (open over 125 years, members over 100) and the parishes ([genealogia.fi](https://www.genealogia.fi/en/genealogy/sources-for-genealogists/parish-registers/)). Its Senate Atlas tiles exist only on Arcanum's commercial service ([Arcanum Finland](https://maps.arcanum.com/en/map/finland1870/)).

**Estonia's 43.9 million-frame Saaga viewer disappears in 2026, and access moves to AIS**, so deep links built today will break ([RA annual report 2025](https://www.ra.ee/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/ra_aruanne_2025.pdf)). Latvia's Raduraksti stops at 1909 and has no API ([JewishGen Latvia](https://latvia.jewishgen.org/research/archival-resources/raduraksti)). Its LVM GEO historical WMS was disconnected after a June 2026 cyberattack ([LV portāls](https://lvportals.lv/dienaskartiba/392002-daleji-atjaunota-lietotnes-lvm-geo-darbiba-2026)). Lithuania's EAIS published 4.09 million images by January 2025 and plans to digitise about 3,100 remaining parish books by 2027, but has no documented API ([LVIA plan](https://lvia.archyvai.lrv.lt/en/news/digitization-plan-20252027-announced-by-the-lithuanian-state-historical-archives-tj/)). Some of its records are in Belarusian and Polish archives, and post-1940 custody is contested among three sources ([FamilySearch Lithuania](https://www.familysearch.org/en/wiki/Lithuania_Church_Records)). Only Sweden (and Denmark, if DigDag's licence allows) has historical parish polygons. For Finland and the Baltics, a parish should be a church point with a "nearest parish of the right denomination" rule, because Baltic congregations were often not territorial. A shared "Russian Empire metrical books" template (Julian dates, consistory duplicates, crown rabbinates) can serve Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania and later the Russian part of Poland.

### Britain and Ireland: Scotland is one rule, England a hundred offices

**Scotland has one holder and one portal.** NRS holds the Church of Scotland Old Parish Registers from 1553 (another source says 1538) to 1854, and ScotlandsPeople searches them free ([NRS OPR coverage](https://nrscotland.gov.uk/learning-and-events/research-guides/coverage-of-the-old-parish-registers)). A register image costs 6 credits (£1.50). Older guides say 5, so the live price needs checking ([Inverclyde guide](https://www.inverclyde.gov.uk/community-life-and-leisure/heritage-services/collections/watt-library/family-history/scotlandspeople)). Civil parishes are OGL according to NRS, although a data.gov.uk copy says "No licence provided" ([spatialdata.gov.scot](https://spatialdata.gov.scot/geonetwork/srv/api/records/d93dbde9-6936-4245-9938-0ef41a5cc0e4)). **England is the hardest UK nation.** Under the 1978 Measure each Church of England diocese designates its own record office ([PRRM 1978](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukcm/1978/2/crossheading/diocesan-record-offices?view=plain)). No machine-readable parish-to-office list was found. The pre-1850 parish GIS by Kain and Oliver is for registered users only ([b2find SN 4348](https://b2find.eudat.eu/dataset/af275acd-85c7-591b-8c3e-8840fa6cc200)). Civil records offer only a free index after login and £8 PDFs of births and deaths, with no marriages online ([GOV.UK prices](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/how-to-order-and-pay-for-certificates/how-to-order-and-pay-for-civil-registration-records-accessible)). Pre-1837 nonconformist registers are at TNA, online by subscription on TheGenealogist ([TNA nonconformists](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/help-with-your-research/research-guides/nonconformists/)). The gazetteer side is strong: OS Open Names and Boundary-Line with historic counties are OGL, and the GB1900 raw dump of historic names is CC0 ([OS Open Names](https://docs.os.uk/os-downloads/addressing-and-location/os-open-names/os-open-names-overview); [Vision of Britain](https://www.visionofbritain.org.uk/data)). NLS historical maps run through MapTiler and need an API key, and commercial use or more than 100,000 tile requests a month need a paid plan ([maptiler.com/nls](https://maptiler.com/nls)).

Ireland is the most open for civil records. irishgenealogy.ie is free without registration, covers the whole island to 1921 and the Republic from 1922, and follows a rolling 100/75/50-year rule ([Irish Genealogy News](https://www.irishgenealogynews.com/2023/03/rolling-years-of-civil-bmd-registers.html)). Church records are the hard part. NLI's Catholic registers are browse-only and mostly end around 1880, although a PRONI guide says 1915 ([NLI](https://registers.nli.ie/about)). About half of the Church of Ireland registers deposited before 1922 burned in the Four Courts fire, or two-thirds of pre-1870 deposits by another estimate ([Irish Genealogy Toolkit](https://www.irish-genealogy-toolkit.com/irish-records-burned.html)). Townlands are CC BY 4.0 from Tailte Éireann or ODbL all-island from townlands.ie ([data.gov.ie](https://data.gov.ie/dataset/townlands-national-statutory-boundaries-ungeneralised-20241); [townlands.ie](https://www.townlands.ie/page/download/)). Historical maps of the Republic are explicitly not open data ([datacatalogue.gov.ie](https://datacatalogue.gov.ie/dataset/historical-maps)). Northern Ireland, by contrast, has **the most open historical maps in scope**: OSNI six-inch County Series sheets under OGL that Rodokmen could re-tile ([OpenDataNI](https://admin.opendatani.gov.uk/dataset/f16e8678-181b-4800-839f-b46584f43dcd)).

## Generate static packs from a reviewed rule registry

The data volumes settle the first architecture question. A 30-country village gazetteer is 6–11 MB gzipped in total and loads per country, so static packs scale. LAU polygons (78–150 MB Europe-wide) and historical overlays need per-country simplification or PMTiles, and the plan's 1 MB-per-file budget already forces splitting large countries by first-level unit. The German minimal gazetteer, for instance, is 3.9 MB raw ([GeoNames dump](https://download.geonames.org/export/dump/)). The research compared four shapes.

| Option                                                 | What it is                                                                                   | Gains                                                                                            | Costs                                                                                                             |
| ------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A. Static packs per country or Land (the current plan) | Each pack ships gazetteer, regions, rules and layers; loaded on demand                       | Offline in reading rooms, no map server, CDN-cacheable, licence provenance per file              | One build per country; cross-border places need several packs; per-country rule authoring                         |
| B. Server-side gazetteer and lookup on the PostGIS VPS | One import of GeoNames, LAU, OSM and historical polygons; `ST_Contains` with date filters    | Europe in one import; time-aware lookups are easy                                                | Breaks offline use and "the map needs no server"; anonymous visitors depend on one VPS; uptime and abuse handling |
| C. Hybrid                                              | Static core plus an optional endpoint for long-tail history                                  | Graceful degradation                                                                             | Two code paths; the endpoint still needs curated data                                                             |
| D. Reviewed rule registry                              | Rules as validated data files keyed by stable place IDs and dates; packs generated from them | The pipeline becomes generic; per-country work becomes data PRs; agents can draft, humans review | Needs a richer rule schema up front                                                                               |

**The recommendation is A as the delivery format and D as the authoring format, with C deferred until a concrete long-tail need appears.** This keeps the plan's ADR direction ("map reference data lives in static Country packs") and its promise that a visitor who stays in the Czech lands downloads nothing more. It also matches how Rodokmen already works: the plan treats "fixing a fact" as "a data PR, not a spec change", and the Tree's ADR 0001 routes contributions through reviewed Proposals. In the registry, each rule line carries its key, period, holders, portal flags and source, and CI checks schema, licence class and link liveness. Agents can draft lines from Archives Portal Europe, Matricula and Wikidata, and a person reviews them. The pan-European research reached the same design, modelled on the curated diocese-and-parish hierarchy Matricula maintains with stable slugs ([Matricula Bestände](https://data.matricula-online.eu/en/bestande/)). GeoNames' daily diff files let the gazetteer part rebuild incrementally ([GeoNames readme](https://download.geonames.org/export/dump/readme.txt)). Option B deserves a fair hearing. Time-aware point-in-polygon queries would be trivial in PostGIS, and the VPS already runs it. But offline search in reading rooms is a core promise. A single EU VPS would become a dependency for every anonymous visitor. And server-side use does not lift licence limits: GISCO stays non-commercial and OSM-derived databases stay share-alike. Because the pack files are plain data, the server can import them later if B ever becomes worth it.

Five refinements make A plus D work at 30 countries. First, **split coverage into two tiers.** A small GeoNames-based search tier for all 28 countries lets search find any European village immediately. Its answer becomes "this place is known; no archive rules yet" instead of "outside every Country pack". Full archive packs then arrive one by one. Second, **share rule templates across borders**: Hungarian civil registration from 1895, Russian Empire metrical books, French-model civil duplicates (France, Belgium, Luxembourg, the Netherlands, the German left bank, Napoleonic Italy), and portal adapters for Matricula, Archion and FamilySearch. Third, **make cross-pack referrals explicit.** The research found many: Luxembourg to Arlon, Koblenz and Moselle; German Ostgebiete notes to the Polish pack via the Landesarchiv Berlin, the EZA and the Catholic military bishop's archive; Danish South Schleswig to Schleswig-Holstein; and Slovak, Romanian, Croatian and Slovene points to Hungarian holdings ([Rigsarkivet South Schleswig](https://www.rigsarkivet.dk/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/Sydslesvigske-kirkeboeger.pdf); [Matricula akmb](https://data.matricula-online.eu/de/deutschland/akmb/)). Fourth, **self-host the basemap** as a Protomaps PMTiles extract on the existing Garage object storage, with per-country low-zoom bundles for offline use. The size of a Europe extract at z12–z14 is an unverified estimate, to be measured with `pmtiles extract --dry-run` ([Protomaps](https://docs.protomaps.com/basemaps/downloads)). Historical overlays follow the same route. Open national WMS/WMTS that pass the 3857-and-licence test are used directly. CC0 or OGL rasters (Sweden, Denmark, Northern Ireland) are re-tiled by Rodokmen. IIIF sheets come through Allmaps. Arcanum, NLS, Bavaria and Mecklenburg-Vorpommern stay links until licensed.

Fifth, **budget for link rot as a recurring cost**, because the churn is constant. Belgium switched off search.arch.be on 23 December 2024, Estonia retires Saaga in 2026, and the Deutsche Digitale Bibliothek turns off API v1 on 31 December 2026 ([DDB](https://pro.deutsche-digitale-bibliothek.de/daten-nutzen/schnittstellen)). Denmark's DAWA is closing, Latvia's map service fell to a cyberattack, and Archion slugs change. Over 30 packs, the weekly link check becomes a maintenance stream. It needs a "bot wall, check by hand" status, or French ADs and Antenati will report as broken every week. This churn also vindicates ADR 0002's private copies of cited pages: the evidence outlives the portal.

## Each answer needs place, faith, kind, period and several holders

The plan's answer shape is `{ region, entries: [{ kind, archive, from, until, note, alsoCheck[] }] }`, where kind is catholic, protestant, greek_catholic, orthodox, jewish or civil. That shape fuses denomination with register kind and allows one archive per entry. The evidence from every region breaks both assumptions. The Nordic researchers summarised the needed key as `(region × denomination × register kind × date range) → [holder, online portal(s), closure years]` with several holders allowed. A Finnish parish, for example, points to Astia before about the 1860s, to SSHY for the 1860s–1920s and to the parish after that ([genealogia.fi](https://www.genealogia.fi/en/genealogy/sources-for-genealogists/parish-registers/)). The UK and German researchers reached the same shape independently.

| Rule field                                                                                                | Why the evidence demands it                                                            | Example                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `denomination` separate from `kind`                                                                       | "Protestant" hides Lutheran, Reformed and Unitarian archives with separate geographies | Leer: Lutheran books on Archion, Reformed books in Leer and not online ([Archion list](https://www.archion.de/de/alle-archive)); NI has five PRONI series by denomination ([PRONI guide](https://www.proni.gov.uk/publications/proni-guide-church-records))                                         |
| `kind` beyond church/civil                                                                                | Population registers, duplicates, transcripts and censuses are first-class sources     | Swedish household examination rolls; Dutch population registers 1850–1920 ([FS NL](https://www.familysearch.org/en/wiki/Netherlands_Population_Registers)); Welsh Bishops' Transcripts at NLW ([NLW](https://www.library.wales/information-for/family-historians/records/church-in-wales-records/)) |
| `holders[]` with a role (original, duplicate, microfilm, finding aid)                                     | One place often has two valid holders                                                  | NRW first copy at the municipality, second copy at LAV NRW ([PA 3103](https://www.archive.nrw.de/sites/default/files/media/files/PA3103_EinleitungDigitalisatpr%C3%A4sentationAugust21.pdf)); Hungarian originals at church archives, microfilm at MNL                                              |
| `from`/`until` per holder, varying inside a region                                                        | Start years differ inside one Land or country                                          | German civil from 1798, 1810, 1850, 1866, 1874 or 1876 ([FS time periods](https://www.familysearch.org/en/wiki/German_Civil_Registration_Time_Periods)); Irish civil split at 1922                                                                                                                  |
| `closure` as a relative rule per event type, plus `onlineUntil`                                           | Legal periods move yearly; portals publish fixed cut-offs that differ from the law     | Hessen online to 1900/1930/1965 against legal 110/80/30 ([Hessen guide](https://landesarchiv.hessen.de/sites/landesarchiv.hessen.de/files/2026-02/recherche_in_den_digitalisierten_personenstandsnebenregistern.pdf))                                                                               |
| Portal flags: `paid`, `login`, `onsiteOnly`, `geoRestricted`, `botWall`, `iiifManifest`, `placeSearchUrl` | Access varies far beyond "paid"                                                        | Agatha login with captcha; Basque search only from Spain; Riksarkivet manifests at `lbiiif.riksarkivet.se/{id}/manifest` ([IIIF wiki](https://github.com/Riksarkivet/dataplattform/wiki/IIIF-english-version))                                                                                      |
| `scope` with unit type and vintage                                                                        | Registers are filed under historical units                                             | Dutch 1812 municipalities; Belgian pre-1977 municipalities; Greek pre-2011 communities; Polish 1900 states; Italian pre-unitary states                                                                                                                                                              |
| `survival`                                                                                                | Some registers no longer exist                                                         | Church of Ireland 1922; Paris état civil burnt 1871 ([fr.wikipedia](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89tat_civil_en_France)); GDR second copies destroyed                                                                                                                                          |
| `languages`, `scripts`, `calendar` defaults                                                               | Members and scan reading need them                                                     | Lithuanian records mainly Russian; Croatian in Glagolitic, Cyrillic and Latin                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| `referrals[]` to other packs                                                                              | Places changed country                                                                 | Former Duchy of Luxembourg; South Schleswig; German Ostgebiete                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| `copyPolicy`                                                                                              | Private copies depend on portal terms                                                  | Matricula CC BY-NC-ND; Riksarkivet Public Domain Mark; Archion metered downloads                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |

The keys must become richer too. Each place should carry a GeoNames ID next to its national code, plus a LAU code where one exists, so sources can be joined; Wikidata links GeoNames IDs to more ([Wikidata](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Wikidata:Licensing)). National codes must come from the country's own scheme, and Eurostat's Greece is `EL`, not ISO's `GR` ([LAU CSV](https://gisco-services.ec.europa.eu/distribution/v2/lau/csv/LAU_RG_01M_2024_4326.csv)). The plan hard-codes code formats in a migration (CZ 6 digits, AT 5, DE 8, PL 7). At 28 countries, it is simpler to validate a code against a pattern the pack declares than to add a database check per country. Where congregations are not territorial (Baltic towns, UK synagogues), the rule unit is a point with "nearest of the right denomination", not a polygon. Closure periods must be stored as rules ("births 100 years after the register closed"), not end years, because they move every January ([NRS](https://nrscotland.gov.uk/research/guides/birth-death-and-marriage-records/statutory-registers-of-births-deaths-and-marriages)). Transfers also lag the rules, because a bound multi-year volume moves only when its last year is free ([Hessen guide](https://landesarchiv.hessen.de/sites/landesarchiv.hessen.de/files/2026-02/recherche_in_den_digitalisierten_personenstandsnebenregistern.pdf)).

### Dates need a calendar, the original text and a year-start style

The Tree stores each date as an EDTF string plus derived earliest and latest day columns (`add-tree-backend` design). **EDTF has no calendar indicator.** It profiles ISO 8601, which is Gregorian, and the Library of Congress spec does not mention Julian dates, dual dating or year start ([LoC EDTF](https://www.loc.gov/standards/datetime/)). GEDCOM 7 has GREGORIAN, JULIAN, FRENCH_R and HEBREW calendars, and since 7.0 it stores slash years as a normalised date plus a PHRASE ([GEDCOM 7](https://gedcom.io/specifications/FamilySearchGEDCOMv7.html)). Gramps adds Swedish, Persian and Islamic calendars, a dual-dated flag and new-year codes (Jan1, Mar1, Mar25, Sep1) ([Gramps 5.2 manual](https://gramps-project.org/wiki/index.php/Gramps_5.2_Wiki_Manual_-_Entering_and_editing_data:_detailed_-_part_1)). Rodokmen therefore needs four additions next to the EDTF value: a calendar identifier, the verbatim date as written, a new-year style and a dual-date flag. The earliest/latest conversion must depend on the place and date of the record, not on today's country. The switch happened in 1582 in Catholic Poland and most of Italy, in 1584 in Austria and Bohemia, in 1700 in Protestant Germany and Denmark, in 1752 in Britain, in 1753 in Sweden and in 1918 in the Russian Empire ([tzdb calendars](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/eggert/tz/main/calendars)). Dutch provinces switched at dates from 1582 to 1701 ([nl.wikipedia](https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregoriaanse_kalender)).

Several traps need explicit tests. **30 February 1712 is a valid Swedish and Finnish date** ([Wikipedia: Swedish calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_calendar)). French Republican dates appear unconverted in registers from late 1793 to 31 December 1805, also in Belgium and Luxembourg, and must be converted with the equinox-based table, not an arithmetic rule ([fr.wikipedia](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calendrier_r%C3%A9publicain); [ANLux](https://anlux.public.lu/fr/rechercher/genealogie.html)). England, Wales and Ireland started the year on 25 March until 1751, while Scotland moved to 1 January in 1600, so their year numbers differ for 1 January–24 March dates between 1600 and 1752 ([NRS calendar](https://www.nrscotland.gov.uk/research/record-guides/old-parish-registers/change-in-calendar); [Calendar Act 1750](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/apgb/Geo2/24/23)). Orthodox church books stay Julian after the civil switch, Finnish government documents carried both styles until 1917, and Courland adopted Gregorian in 1617 and reverted in 1796 ([Wikipedia: Gregorian adoption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adoption_of_the_Gregorian_calendar)).

### Name forms need a language tag, not a type per language

The plan adds `pl` and `ru` place-name languages and `polish` and `russian` name types next to the existing `latin` and `german` types. It rejected a single "language form" type with a language field because of its ripple into `add-vault-import`. **At 24 EU languages plus Latin, Church Slavonic, Yiddish and regional languages, one type per language does not scale.** GEDCOM 7 already models this: a name "as written in the culture of the individual", plus TRAN transliterations that must carry a BCP 47 language tag ([GEDCOM 7](https://gedcom.io/specifications/FamilySearchGEDCOMv7.html)). Since `add-vault-import` has not started, the cleaner model costs least now. The name model also needs per-person display order. CLDR treats Hungarian as customarily surname-first, stores patronymics as `given2` in most Slavic languages, and uses `surname2` for Spanish-style double surnames ([UTS #35 Person Names](https://www.unicode.org/reports/tr35/tr35-personNames.html)). It needs gendered surname forms too: Czech law has allowed women to register a surname without "-ová" since a 2022 amendment ([zakonyprolidi.cz 301/2000](https://www.zakonyprolidi.cz/cs/2000-301)). And it must handle Scandinavian transitions. Danish patronymics were fixed as surnames by an 1857 law ([FamilySearch Denmark Names](https://www.familysearch.org/en/wiki/Denmark_Names,_Personal)). Swedish soldier names belonged to the post, not the family, and Dalarna farm names come before the given name ([FamilySearch Sweden Names](https://www.familysearch.org/en/wiki/Sweden_Names,_Personal)).

Place names need historical aliases with periods: Magyarised forms, German, Swedish, Polish and Russian exonyms such as Reval/Tallinn, Mitau/Jelgava and Kowno/Kaunas, and pre-merger municipality names. No open multilingual alias table was found for the Baltics, where the same record set can be in German, Russian or Estonian ([RA guide](https://ra.ee/vau/index.php/en/page/article/index?menuId=16)). The usable building blocks are national: GB1900 (CC0 raw dump), FranceArchives places aligned to GeoNames, INSEE's commune history since 1943, ISTAT's since 1991 and gemeentegeschiedenis.nl ([FranceArchives lieux](https://www.data.gouv.fr/datasets/portail-francearchives-les-lieux-des-archives); [INSEE COG](https://www.insee.fr/fr/information/8740222); [ISTAT codes](https://www.istat.it/classificazione/codici-dei-comuni-delle-province-e-delle-regioni/)). Search folding must grow beyond the plan's letter table. Full Unicode case folding is needed (ß to "ss", Greek final ς to σ) ([CaseFolding.txt](https://www.unicode.org/Public/UCD/latest/ucd/CaseFolding.txt)). So are cross-script transliterations with several variants, such as ICU's Bulgarian-Latin/BGN and Greek-Latin ([ICU transforms](https://unicode-org.github.io/icu/userguide/transforms/general/)), and, for person names, phonetic matching such as Beider–Morse ([stevemorse.org](https://stevemorse.org/phonetics/bmpm.htm)).

The other Tree enums widen in the same way. Parish Denomination needs at least Lutheran, Reformed, Unitarian, Anglican, Presbyterian, Church of Ireland, Old Believer, Mennonite and Quaker next to the plan's five values. These all appear as separate holdings in the notes ([FamilySearch Latvia](https://www.familysearch.org/en/wiki/Latvia_Church_Records); [Archion Mennonites](https://www.archion.de/de/alle-archive/rheinland-pfalz/archiv-der-mennonitischen-forschungsstelle); [TNA nonconformists](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/help-with-your-research/research-guides/nonconformists/)). SourceType needs population register, duplicate or transcript, and census next to the new civil register. Content in Hebrew and Yiddish community records needs bidi isolation (`dir="auto"`, `<bdi>`) even though no interface language is right-to-left ([W3C bidi](https://www.w3.org/International/articles/inline-bidi-markup/)).

## Sharing the Tree, not mapping Europe, changes the legal footing

The map's packs hold reference data about archives and places, not personal data, so pan-European expansion of the map is a licensing question only. The Tree is different. Expansion either keeps one family per installation or turns Rodokmen into a service for many families, and **that choice decides which privacy law applies.** This section describes rules and sources and is not legal advice; several legal findings come from statute text that was not re-fetched this session because EUR-Lex refused automated access.

### The household exemption fits one family, not a service

GDPR Art. 2(2)(c) excludes processing "by a natural person in the course of a purely personal or household activity". The last sentence of Recital 18 adds that the Regulation "applies to controllers or processors which provide the means for processing personal data for such personal or household activities" ([GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj)). The UK GDPR uses the same wording ([legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/2)). The CJEU reads "purely" narrowly. Internet publication to "an indefinite number of people" is not household use (_Lindqvist_) ([C-101/01](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:62001CJ0101)), and a home camera covering a public space is not either (_Ryneš_) ([C-212/13](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:62013CJ0212)). A community that organises its members' data collection can be a joint controller without ever accessing the data (_Jehovan todistajat_) ([C-25/17](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:62017CJ0025)). The Article 29 Working Party weighs five factors together: dissemination to an indefinite audience, data about people unrelated to the poster, professional scale, collective organisation, and adverse impact ([WP29 Annex 2](https://ec.europa.eu/justice/article-29/documentation/other-document/files/2013/20130227_statement_dp_annex2_en.pdf)).

For Rodokmen the consequences are concrete. A self-hosted instance per family, run by a family member, with passkey login restricted to relatives, sits best within the exemption. Its exposure grows as the Member list grows to hundreds of extended relatives who do not know each other. Anything personal shown without login is the _Lindqvist_ scenario, so the public map must stay free of personal data. A shared multi-family service puts the operator inside the GDPR whatever each family's status. Cross-family matching, shared search or AI reading would make the operator a controller. That brings a lawful basis, **Art. 9 duties because parish registers record religion**, notices, data-subject rights, a DPIA and processor contracts. Recital 160 treats genealogical research as historical research under Art. 89, which helps with derogations but does not remove these duties ([GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj)). No EDPB, CNIL, Garante, AEPD or ICO guidance specific to online family trees was found. For hosting, the EU renewed the UK adequacy decisions on 19 December 2025, valid until 27 December 2031, so one EU VPS can serve UK families ([Freeths](https://www.freeths.co.uk/insights-events/legal-articles/2026/european-commission-renews-uk-adequacy-decisions-until-27-december-2031/)).

Accessibility follows the same split. The European Accessibility Act covers a closed list of consumer services, including e-commerce "with a view to concluding a consumer contract", and exempts service-providing microenterprises (fewer than 10 staff, €2 million or less) ([EAA](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/882/oj); [Taylor Wessing](https://www.taylorwessing.com/en/interface/2025/accessibility/key-eu-accessibility-act-exemptions-and-the-challenges-they-pose)). A free family instance sits outside it, and a paid shared service run by a microenterprise is exempt. The UK Equality Act 2010 s.29, by contrast, covers any service to "the public or a section of the public (for payment or not)" ([Equality Act s.29](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2010/15/section/29)). That makes WCAG 2.2 a sound baseline for a shared service in either case.

### Living must become two per-country parameters

Rodokmen counts a person as Living when born within the last 100 years with no recorded death, or with neither birth nor death recorded (`GLOSSARY.md`). That matches the UK 1939 Register rule ([TNA 1939 Register](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/help-with-your-research/research-guides/1939-register/)) and most 100-year birth closures. **It is less strict than Germany's 110-year birth closure** ([PStG §5](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/pstg/__5.html)), and Hungary reportedly uses 110 years as well ([HVG](https://hvg.hu/tudomany/20251221_mnl-aol-leveltar-csaladfakutatas-anyakonyv-keziras-felismeressel)). Closure periods are archive-access proxies, though. The GDPR protects every living person regardless of age, and people over 100 may still be alive ([Genealogie Online](https://www.genealogieonline.nl/help/item.php/236)).

A second axis sits outside the GDPR. Recital 27 excludes the deceased and lets member states legislate ([GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj)). **Denmark applies the full GDPR to people dead for less than 10 years** ([danskelove.dk §2](https://danskelove.dk/databeskyttelsesloven/2)). Hungary gives authorised persons or close relatives rights for 5 years after death ([NAIH](https://naih.hu/files/Ajanlas_online-adatok-halal-utani-sorsarol.pdf)). Italy and Spain let relatives exercise rights with no fixed end date ([Codice privacy art. 2-terdecies](https://brocardi.it/codice-della-privacy/parte-i/titolo-i/capo-iii/art2terdecies.html); [LOPDGDD](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2018-16673)). The model therefore needs **a likely-living threshold and a post-death protection window**, set per Tree by the Owner, with presets per country. A single 110-year birth threshold would be at least as strict as every closure found for births. The window matters only in a shared service or a Danish, Hungarian or Estonian family, and it must handle relatives' access and erasure requests.

### Private scan copies rest on four layers of rights

ADR 0002 keeps a private image of every cited page on the premise that "archives allow copies for personal research but not republication". That premise is Czech and does not carry over unchanged. Rights come in four layers. The old registers themselves are public domain. Scans can carry a related right in "simple photographs": 50 years under Germany's §72 UrhG, 20 under Italy's art. 87 and 25 under Spain's art. 128. DSM Directive Art. 14 removes such rights only for reproductions of public-domain _visual art_, and whether handwritten registers count is untested ([Commons PD-Art](https://c.enwp.org/wiki/Commons:Reuse_of_PD-Art_photographs); [DSM](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32019L0790); [BOE TRLPI](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1996-8930)). Electronic indexes can carry the 15-year sui generis database right, whose private-use exception covers only non-electronic databases ([Database Directive](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:31996L0009)). On top of copyright, archive terms apply. Italian cultural-heritage law charges reproduction fees for profit-making use ([DM 161/2023](https://www.sissco.it/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/DM161A.pdf)), and Antenati reserves publication of its images to the Directorate-General. **The brief's claim that Antenati is CC0 was not confirmed and is probably wrong** ([Circolare Antenati 2026](https://archivi.cultura.gov.it/fileadmin/risorse/attivita/circolari/2026/04_aprile/22_FamilySearch/Circolare_Antenati_2026-signed.pdf)).

Private copying itself is an optional InfoSoc exception that varies by state ([InfoSoc](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32001L0029)). **The UK has no general private-copying exception**, since s.28B CDPA was quashed in 2015 ([BASCA, BAILII](https://www.bailii.org/ew/cases/EWHC/Admin/2015/2041.html)). For UK sources, private copies must rest on licence terms such as OGL or explicit permissions. Portals differ widely. Riksarkivet's Public Domain Mark is the best case ([API terms](https://sok.riksarkivet.se/en/data-api/api/anvandarvillkor-api)). Matricula's CC BY-NC-ND permits non-commercial copies but forbids commercial reuse ([Matricula terms](https://data.matricula-online.eu/en/nutzungsbedingungen/)). Archion meters downloads at 50 per monthly pass ([Archion prices](https://www.archion.de/de/zugang-kaufen)). Agatha marks its manifests "AGR Copyright" ([Agatha](https://agatha.arch.be/en/search/genealogie/)). Hence the `copyPolicy` field per portal, shown when a Member attaches a page. A shared service that stores scans and serves them to other families moves from private copying into communication to the public, which no private-copy exception covers. Copies must therefore stay scoped to the family's own Tree.

### AI scan reading sends religion-bearing pages to the US under SCCs

ADR 0011 already makes scan reading opt-in by the Owner. Expansion sharpens two facts. **Anthropic's DPA (effective 24 February 2025) relies on the EU Standard Contractual Clauses, Modules 2 and 3, with the UK and Swiss addenda, not on the EU–US Data Privacy Framework.** The customer is controller, Anthropic is processor, there is no data-residency commitment, and sub-processors come with a 15-day objection window ([Anthropic DPA](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/data-processing-addendum)). The first-party API's `inference_geo` accepts only `"us"` or `"global"`, with US-only at 1.1× the price. The parameter is unavailable on Bedrock, Vertex and Foundry. Vertex lists an `"eu"` multi-region, but model availability there was not checked. These details come from Anthropic's SDK reference bundled with Claude Code rather than a fetched web page, so they need confirming against the platform docs ([Anthropic platform docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/claude-in-amazon-bedrock)).

The DPF survived the General Court in _Latombe_ on 3 September 2025. The appeal C‑703/25 P is pending, and the PCLOB, an oversight body the DPF relies on, lost its quorum in January 2025 ([WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/insights/blogs/wilmerhale-privacy-and-cybersecurity-law/20251201-european-court-of-justice-to-review-challenge-to-eu-us-data-privacy-framework); [CDT](https://cdt.org/insights/what-the-pclob-firings-mean-for-the-eu-us-data-privacy-framework/)). A ruling against the DPF would not remove the SCCs. It would weaken the US-law assessment that SCC transfer impact assessments rely on under _Schrems II_ ([C-311/18](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:62018CJ0311); [EDPB 01/2020](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/our-work-tools/our-documents/recommendations/recommendations-012020-measures-supplement-transfer_en)). A family instance under the household exemption is the least exposed. A shared-service operator would be the controller in Anthropic's DPA and need its own transfer assessment. Two mitigations follow from the data. Send only crops, and send only pages older than the applicable closure period, which cuts the share of living people on the page. There is also an alternative: portals now read scans themselves. Archion launched AI handwriting recognition on 22 April 2026 ([Archion](https://www.archion.de/en/discover-archion/news/all-news/text-recognition-on-archion)), the Hungarian National Archives added it for Pest county ([HVG](https://hvg.hu/tudomany/20251221_mnl-aol-leveltar-csaladfakutatas-anyakonyv-keziras-felismeressel)), and Riksarkivet is building IIIF Content Search for transcribed text ([Riksarkivet APIs](https://sok.riksarkivet.se/en/data-api/api)). The EU AI Act's transparency duties were not researched.

### Maps and data licences decide what a pack may store offline

Every pack source needs a licence class recorded in `pack.json`, and the build must refuse sources incompatible with the Owner's commercial choice. **Permissive sources** are GeoNames, ISTAT, CNIG, CAOP, Finland's NLS, Tailte Éireann and Lithuania's register (CC BY 4.0); Lantmäteriet, Luxembourg, BAG, Wikidata and the GB1900 raw dump (CC0); OS, NRS and OSNI (OGL); France (Licence Ouverte); and BKG (dl-de/by-2-0). BKG requires a change notice when data is modified ([BKG VG250](https://gdz.bkg.bund.de/index.php/default/verwaltungsgebiete-1-250-000-stand-01-01-vg250-01-01.html)). **Share-alike sources** are OSM and townlands.ie (ODbL) and SLUB's pre-1900 sheets (CC BY-SA). Their derived files must sit in separate layers under the same licence ([SLUB Kartenforum](https://search.kartenforum.slub-dresden.de/vk20/_search)). **Non-commercial sources** are GISCO LAU, MPIDR Mosaic, HISGIS, Rumsey items and Matricula images. GOV is reported CC BY-ND, which forbids derived files. **Unknown licences** cover Matricula's parish GeoJSON, gemeentegeschiedenis.nl, DigDag, Statbel and the historical layers of Flanders, Wallonia, Saarland and Luxembourg; these need asking. Denmark adds its own licence plus a mandatory account and token ([Danish terms](https://www.sdfe.dk/Media/638052180760639799/Vilk%C3%A5r%20for%20brug%20af%20frie%20geografiske%20data,%20opdateret%20november%202022.pdf)).

Offline caching of third-party map services is a separate question from embedding them. The OSMF forbids it outright. IGN France's terms were not read for prefetch rules. Italy's IGM WMS should be treated as online-only until its licence is clear ([PCN WMS](http://wms.pcn.minambiente.it/ogc?map=/ms_ogc/WMS_v1.3/raster/IGM_25000.map&service=wms&request=getCapabilities&version=1.3.0)). MapTiler's pricing page states no offline terms, and Stadia offers perpetual offline licensing only to enterprise customers ([Stadia pricing](https://stadiamaps.com/pricing/)). Self-tiled CC0 and OGL rasters, plus a self-hosted basemap, are therefore the only overlays a "download for offline" button can safely include.

## Twenty-four interface languages matter less than record languages

The EU has 24 official languages in three scripts (Latin, Greek and Cyrillic), all left-to-right ([europa.eu](https://european-union.europa.eu/principles-countries-history/languages_en)). The UK adds Welsh, official in Wales, Scottish Gaelic, and Irish, official in Northern Ireland since 2022 ([Welsh Measure 2011](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/mwa/2011/1); [Gaelic Act 2005](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/asp/2005/7); [Identity and Language Act 2022](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2022/45)). Statutory duties for Welsh fall on public bodies, not on a family app. The plan keeps the interface Czech and English, with pack, place and archive names in their own language. That stays workable through Phase 2 of the roadmap below, because a reader looking up a Swedish parish already reads the archive's Swedish name. Locale formats come for free from CLDR via the browser's `Intl` APIs ([CLDR](https://cldr.unicode.org/)). Collation needs locale tailoring for sorted lists, such as Swedish å, ä and ö after z and Czech "ch" after h ([UTS #10](https://www.unicode.org/reports/tr10/)). Interface languages beyond Czech and English should follow the countries that ship. German comes first (Austria, Germany, Luxembourg), then Polish and Slovak. That ordering is a recommendation from the roadmap, not something the notes measured.

The heavier localisation load is in the records. German registers use Kurrent handwriting and Fraktur print, with Latin in Catholic books, French west of the Rhine under French rule and Danish in Schleswig-Holstein until 1864 ([FamilySearch Germany languages](https://www.familysearch.org/en/wiki/Germany_Language_and_Languages)). Slovak records before the 1900s are "mostly in Latin and Hungarian" ([FamilySearch Slovakia](https://www.familysearch.org/en/wiki/Slovakia_Church_Records)). Croatian ones mix Latin, Croatian, Hungarian and Italian in Glagolitic, Cyrillic and Roman scripts ([FamilySearch Croatia](https://www.familysearch.org/en/wiki/Croatia_Church_Records)). Finnish church books were in Swedish until the end of the 19th century ([genealogia.fi](https://www.genealogia.fi/en/genealogy/sources-for-genealogists/parish-registers/)). Estonian ones are in German, Russian or Estonian ([RA guide](https://ra.ee/vau/index.php/en/page/article/index?menuId=16)). Lithuanian records are mainly Russian, with Lithuanian only in the 20th century ([FamilySearch Lithuania](https://www.familysearch.org/en/wiki/Lithuania_Church_Records)). Maltese parish books use Italian name forms ([FamilySearch Malta](https://www.familysearch.org/en/wiki/Malta_Church_Records)). Each rule should therefore state its record languages and scripts. The place sheet can then tell a Member what to expect, portal hints can warn that AIS's search form is Estonian-only or that ISSA is Bulgarian-only, and scan reading can be switched on per pack only after the `add-scan-reading` benchmark passes for that pack's scripts. The plan already defers Russian and Polish handwriting to that benchmark, and Europe adds Kurrent, Hungarian, Glagolitic, Greek and Bulgarian Cyrillic to the list.

### What the notes could not settle

Several facts that affect the design conflict across sources or remain unverified, and each needs a check before the rule that depends on it ships. Belgian death closure is 50 years by the Civil Code and Agatha, but 75 per Wallonia and Luxembourg's Conseil d'État ([Agatha help](https://agatha.arch.be/help/beginning/); [CdE opinion](https://wdocs-pub.chd.lu/docs/Dossiers_parlementaires/8516/20260721_Avis.pdf)). Estonia's post-mortem rule is 10 or 30 years. Hungary's 86-year marriage closure is unusual. Croatia's archive cut-off is 1857 or 1860. NLI's Catholic registers end around 1880 or 1915. The Dutch civil start is 1811, not the brief's 1796, and Belgium's is 1796 or 1797 ([arch.be 2021](http://www.arch.be/index.php?l=fr&m=actualites&r=toutes-les-actualites&a=2021-04-01-la-numerisation-et-l-ouverture-a-la-recherche-de-l-etat-civil-un-arrete-royal-tant-attendu.-quelles-en-sont-les-implications)). Baden's Standesbücher end in 1869 or 1870, and Hildesheim's burials close after 40 or 100 years ([BAH PDF](https://www.bistum-hildesheim.de/fileadmin/dateien/Bilder/bistumsarchiv/BAH_Kirchenbuecher_2023-02-23.pdf)). Bulgaria's archive structure is six regional archives with 21 centres or 28 districts, and Lithuania's post-1940 civil custodian has three candidates. On licences: EuroBoundaryMap is described both as open and as non-open; the GOV licence rests on a search snippet; Swedish place names are CC0 per Lantmäteriet but CC BY 4.0 per the OSM import page ([OSM Sweden import](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Import/Catalogue/Sweden_Place_Name_Import)); Scottish civil parishes are OGL or carry no licence, depending on the catalogue; and Hamburg's historical maps carry both dl-de/by-2-0 and an INSPIRE restriction. Counts and prices also disagree. Antenati's 2025 additions are 7.3 million or 53.6 million images ([Gazzetta di Parma](https://www.gazzettadiparma.it/italia-mondo/2026/04/17/news/archivi-di-stato-sul-portale-antenati-quasi-2-milioni-di-utenti-da-219-paesi-937292/)). OpenArchieven reports 371 million observations from over 200 archives or 362 million from 180 ([llms.txt](https://www.openarchieven.nl/llms.txt); [brand page](https://www.openarchieven.nl/brand/)). GRONI credits and ScotlandsPeople image prices differ between official pages and older guides. Two Danish DAWA shutdown dates are reported. The Bulgarian, Romanian and Greek calendar switch dates are confirmed only through Wikipedia, and Anthropic's `inference_geo` behaviour comes from an SDK reference rather than a public page.

## Conclusion

Taken together, the research shows that **Europe is a curation and governance problem, not a mapping problem.** The bytes are small and mostly open. What scales badly is human knowledge of custody: which of a German town's three church systems and two civil copies holds an 1880 baptism, or whether a Transylvanian register sits in Cluj or Budapest. That knowledge decays as portals migrate, merge and fall over. The pack format is right, but its centre of gravity must move from geometry to a reviewed, dated, multi-holder rule registry with an explicit maintenance budget. The countries that test that registry best are not Rodokmen's neighbours but its opposites: Sweden for stable IIIF deep links, France for rules without links, and Scotland for a single holder under a different legal system.

The second shift is that the Tree, not the map, carries the legal risk. As long as each family runs its own instance, the household exemption, Czech-style private copies and opt-in AI reading remain defensible with modest changes: a stricter or configurable Living threshold, a copy policy per portal, and closure-aware filtering before any page leaves for the US. The moment Rodokmen becomes a shared service for many families, it becomes a controller of religious data across 28 post-mortem regimes. Several of the free licences that make the packs cheap also stop applying, because GISCO and Matricula images are non-commercial and the free MapTiler and Stadia tiers forbid commercial use. That is why "one family or many" is the first decision the Owner has to make, before any further country pack is built.
